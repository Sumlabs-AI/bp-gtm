1. PRODUCT SUMMARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

One-liner:
  A GTM tool that tells Base's sales team WHERE to knock, WHEN to knock,
  and WHAT to say, by scoring every neighborhood in Texas.

Users:
  - Sales reps (door-to-door): map + weekly priority feed + talk tracks
  - Sales managers: region comparison, estimated buyers, revenue potential
  - (Future) Utility partners like CoServ: where to promote their Base program

Product surfaces:
  1. Map: neighborhoods (Census block groups) colored by Priority score,
     with toggleable layers (propensity, need, solar homes, untapped, etc.)
  2. Neighborhood card: score breakdown, key stats, top reasons (SHAP),
     generated talk track
  3. "This week" feed: neighborhoods boosted by timing signals
     (storm/freeze alerts, recent outages, ERCOT grid stress)
  4. Segment filters: solar homes without battery, new homeowners
  5. Manager view: estimated backup buyers and revenue potential by region

Pitch angle:
  Most sales maps answer WHO. This answers WHO (propensity model),
  WHEN (timing signals) and WHAT TO SAY (talk tracks built from real
  outage history).

2. SCOPE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

- Platform: Texas only (Base's core market; ERCOT data is Texas-only).
- Demo metro: DFW (deregulated, large suburbs, CoServ partner territory).
  Alternative: Houston.
- Training cities: cities with usable permit open data. Candidates:
  Austin, Dallas, San Antonio, Fort Worth, Houston. [VERIFY each]
- Prediction area: all Texas block groups (features cover the whole state).

Why prediction still matters with permit cities:
  - Permit data covers city limits only. Suburbs (Plano, Frisco, McKinney,
    Arlington, Denton, Round Rock, unincorporated Harris County, CoServ
    territory...) are NOT covered. Roughly three-quarters of the people in
    the 4 big metros live outside those city limits.
  - Suburbs = owner-occupied single-family = likely Base's best market.
  - Inside permit cities, the model gives "untapped" gaps
    (predicted demand vs actual installs).

Austin nuance: Austin Energy territory is not deregulated; Base sells there
via utility partnership. Austin permits are still fine for TRAINING
(they show who buys backup).

3. UNIT OF ANALYSIS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

- Census block group (2020 boundaries), ~600-3,000 people each.
- Every dataset is brought to block group level:
    address-level data (permits, parcels) -> geocode -> count/aggregate
    block group data (ACS) -> direct
    tract-only ACS tables -> copied to each block group in the tract
    county-level data (outages, weather) -> copied to each block group
- Product stays at block group level: no household-level profiles shown
  to reps (privacy; judges will notice).
- IMPORTANT: use 2020 block group boundaries everywhere (TIGER/Line 2020+).
  Do not mix 2010 and 2020 geographies.

5. BASE INSTALL REQUIREMENTS -> INSTALLABILITY PROXY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Published Base requirements (from Base help center):
  - Battery outside, within 20 ft of the electric meter
  - No trenching or attic conduit runs
  - 3 ft clearance from gas meters, AC units, fences, obstructions
  - Not indoors or in garages
  - Main breaker 100-200A (Austin: 150-200A; dual battery: 200A)
  - Home draw must be <= 11 kW for the battery to turn on
  - HOAs cannot block it in Texas (Property Code 202.010)

Public proxies:
  - Panel capacity -> year built (old homes often 100A), panel upgrade permits
  - Outdoor space -> gap between building footprint and parcel lines;
    townhomes / attached homes harder
  - Power draw -> home size, electric heating
  - Single-family owner-occupied -> property type + homestead exemption

Output: "installable share" per block group = % homes likely to meet
requirements.
  - Used as ML feature (explains low permit counts: demand exists but
    homes can't host a battery)
  - Used as multiplier in the Priority score


----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. LABEL CONSTRUCTION (PERMITS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Typical permit fields (varies by city):
  permit type/subtype, free-text work description, address (sometimes
  coordinates), declared job value, dates (applied/issued/final),
  contractor name, status.

Step 1 - Classify permits (often NOT a structured field):
  a) Keyword rules first: generator, Generac, Kohler, ATS, transfer switch,
     Powerwall, battery, ESS, energy storage, solar, PV
  b) LLM for ambiguous descriptions: classify generator / battery /
     solar / panel upgrade / other + extract brand & capacity if present
  c) Hand-check ~100 samples; report classification accuracy

Step 2 - Geocode and assign to 2020 block groups.

Step 3 - Choose time window:
  - Plot permits per year first. Winter Storm Uri (Feb 2021) likely caused
    a spike; the label could be dominated by one event.
  - Consider post-Uri years, or include year effects.
  - Market shift: federal 25D residential battery tax credit expired
    end of 2025 -> adoption patterns may change.

Step 4 - Generator vs battery test:
  - Compute generator rate and battery rate per block group.
  - Strong correlation -> combine into one label (backup intent).
  - Weak correlation -> battery = label, generator = feature.

Step 5 - Final label:
  LABEL = (generator + battery installs) per 1,000 eligible homes
          per block group, over the chosen window.
  Solar = feature + segment (not label, to avoid learning "solar
  buyers", driven by roof/sun/eco motivation).

Status field: check for withdrawn / expired applications. Possible extra
signal (intent without follow-through). Denied permits rarely published.

Base's own installs:
  Search contractor field for "Base Power". [VERIFY in first hour]
  Use for VALIDATION + map layer, NOT as training label, because Base
  installs reflect where Base has sold (sales coverage bias), there is
  less data, and Base's cheap offer attracts a broader profile.
  Future: fine-tune on Base customers once volume is large.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
7. ML MODEL: PROPENSITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Question: which neighborhoods tend to buy backup power?
Target: backup installs per 1,000 eligible homes (block group)
Features: Fit features (ACS, parcel aggregates, installability, solar
  rate, density). NOT outage data (kept in Need score to avoid
  double-counting and to keep "who" separate from "pain").
Model: LightGBM regression
  - Poisson objective with exposure = number of eligible homes
    (or predict log rate)
  - Monotonic constraints where logic is clear (e.g. % owner-occupied
    should never lower propensity)
  - Keep 20-40 features; watch for overfitting (modest sample)
Explainability: SHAP values -> top reasons per neighborhood -> talk tracks

Leakage rules:
  - Never use features built from the same permits as the label
    (e.g. "generator permits last year" for a generator label)
  - Neighbor adoption features only from periods BEFORE the label window

Output uses:
  - Propensity percentile rank -> Priority score
  - Predicted rate x eligible homes -> estimated backup buyers
  - Predicted vs actual (permit cities) -> untapped areas


----------------------------------------------------------------------
8. VALIDATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Baselines (the model MUST beat these):
  1. Income-only ranking (sort by median household income)
  2. Hand-built Fit formula (owner-occupancy, single-family, value)
  3. (If temporal) previous-period permit rate

Validation schemes:
  a) Spatial CV: hold out whole tracts / zip codes (neighbors look alike;
     random splits inflate accuracy)
  b) Leave-one-city-out: train Austin + San Antonio, test Dallas, etc.
     Tests transfer to new areas.
  c) Temporal (strongest): train on permits through 2023, predict
     2024-2025 installs. "The model predicted next year's adoption."
  d) Base installs: "Our top 20% of neighborhoods contain X% of Base's
     installs." Only evaluate inside areas Base actually serves.

Metrics:
  - Lift / capture curve: top 10% of neighborhoods capture X% of installs
    (main demo metric, easy for sales and judges)
  - Rank metrics WITHIN each city (permit enforcement differs by city)
  - Poisson deviance for model tuning

Error analysis: show where the model is wrong and why.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
9. SCORING FORMULA
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  Priority = Eligibility x Installable x (Propensity^0.6 x Need^0.4)
  Feed score = Priority x Timing

Components:
  Eligibility  (0 or 1): in Base service area (direct or utility partner)
  Installable  (0-1): share of homes likely meeting install requirements
  Propensity   (0-1): ML prediction, percentile rank within metro
  Need         (0-1): percentile rank of historical outage hours +
                      severe weather events
  Timing       (1.0-1.5): rules
                 x1.5 storm/freeze alert within 72h
                 x1.2 outage in last 14 days (or ERCOT grid stress)
                 x1.0 otherwise

Design choices:
  - Percentile ranks put components on the same scale
  - Multiplication: an area needs BOTH right households and real pain
  - Weights 0.6/0.4 adjustable; ask Base what matters more
  - Timing only in the weekly feed; main map stays stable

Example: Propensity 0.9, Need 0.7, fully eligible/installable
  -> Priority ~0.81; with ice storm alert -> feed score ~1.2

Untapped flag (permit cities):
  - Do NOT use raw predicted - actual (noisy in small areas)
  - Use shrinkage: blend observed rate toward prediction, more blending
    where sample is small; or flag areas where actual installs are
    statistically well below expected (Poisson test)


----------------------------------------------------------------------
10. MANAGER VIEW: MARKET SIZING & REVENUE POTENTIAL
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  Estimated backup buyers = predicted rate x eligible installable homes
  Revenue potential = estimated buyers x value per customer

Rules:
  - Value per customer = adjustable input. Default = ERCOT trading
    estimate; note Base should plug in real lifetime value (install fee,
    monthly fee, energy margin, ancillary services, utility payments,
    minus costs)
  - Show a range (low / mid / high), not one number
  - Aggregate by metro/region (where grid value actually differs)
  - Do NOT multiply the Priority score by dollars: it is a rank, not a
    probability; within a metro grid value is constant (no ranking change)

Grid value calculation (per load zone):
  1. Download 1+ years real-time load zone prices (gridstatus)
  2. Battery: 39.2 kWh, ~20% homeowner reserve (~31 kWh tradable),
     11 kW max rate, ~90% round-trip efficiency [VERIFY with Base]
  3. One cycle/day: charge in cheapest 15-min intervals, discharge in
     most expensive ones
  4. Profit = discharge revenue - charge cost / efficiency; sum per year
  Caveat: perfect foresight -> UPPER BOUND ("maximum trading value").
  Ancillary services excluded (same price statewide).
  Load zones: Houston, North, South, West, Austin Energy, CPS, LCRA,
  Rayburn. Most of DFW = North.

11. RISKS & MITIGATIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Label measures permit behavior, not only demand
   (enforcement differs by city/time)
   -> Evaluate ranking within each city; city effects

2. Model just learns "rich neighborhoods"
   -> Always compare to income-only baseline; report honestly

3. Timing: Uri spike, 25D credit expiry, ACS windows vs permit years
   -> Plot permits by year; choose window deliberately

4. Base installs biased by sales coverage
   -> Validate only inside Base-served areas

5. Noisy untapped gaps in small block groups
   -> Shrinkage / statistical test; show confidence

6. Data engineering eats the weekend (geocoding, parsing, boundaries,
   appraisal formats) - MOST LIKELY risk
   -> Freeze data by Saturday noon; formula-first fallback

7. Proxy mismatch: generator/Powerwall buyers vs Base buyers
   -> Generator vs battery correlation test; Base install validation

8. Census margins of error at block group level
   -> Fewer, robust features; show low-confidence areas

9. County-level outage data: same Need score within a county
   -> Acknowledge; propensity provides neighborhood-level detail

10. Fairness: score may steer reps away from lower-income areas
    (redlining concern; utility partners have equity goals)
    -> Show feature drivers; affordability-program filter; be ready
       to answer

11. Feedback loop once deployed: new installs cluster where the tool
    sent reps; retraining reinforces bias
    -> Mention in pitch; hold-out exploration areas in future

12. Training on actual history but using live signals in production
    -> Timing is rule-based, not ML, so limited impact
